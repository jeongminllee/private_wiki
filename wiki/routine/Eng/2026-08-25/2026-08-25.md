# Day 22 — Explaining Confidence Without Sounding Either Too Certain or Too Weak

오늘 목표는 **결과에 어느 정도 확신이 있는지 정확하게 표현하는 것**입니다. C1 수준에서는 `I think`, `maybe`, `probably`만 반복하지 않고, **evidence → confidence → uncertainty → conclusion**을 구분해서 말할 수 있어야 합니다.

## 1. Speaking Prompt — 2분

다음 상황을 생각하고 60–90초 동안 영어로 답해보세요.

> Your experiment shows that a new method improves accuracy by about 2 percentage points, but the result varies somewhat across runs. How confident are you that the method is genuinely better?

답변에는 **관찰된 결과, 확신을 높이는 근거, 아직 남아 있는 불확실성, 현재 가능한 결론**을 포함하세요.

시작은 간단하게 해도 좋습니다.

> The results are encouraging, but I would not describe the improvement as conclusive yet.

---

## 2. Reusable Speaking Templates — 3분

첫 번째는 **확신의 수준을 조절**할 때 바로 사용할 수 있습니다.

> I am reasonably confident that **[claim]**, because **[evidence]**.

예:

> I am reasonably confident that the method improves performance because the same trend appeared across several runs.

`I am sure`보다 훨씬 신중하고 전문적입니다.

두 번째는 **불확실성을 인정하면서 주장을 유지**하는 구조입니다.

> There is still some uncertainty regarding **[issue]**, particularly because **[reason]**.

예:

> There is still some uncertainty regarding the size of the improvement, particularly because performance varies across random seeds.

세 번째는 **현재 근거로 가능한 결론의 범위**를 말할 때 유용합니다.

> Based on the available evidence, I would conclude that **[moderate claim]**, rather than **[overstated claim]**.

예:

> Based on the available evidence, I would conclude that the method is promising, rather than claiming that it is consistently superior.

---

## 3. Useful Words & Expressions — 4분

### 1. reasonably confident

**상당한 근거를 가지고 어느 정도 확신하는**

> I am reasonably confident that the effect is real.

일상 영어에서도 자연스럽습니다.

> I'm reasonably confident that we can finish the project this week.

`very sure`보다 더 차분하고 분석적으로 들립니다.

### 2. conclusive

**결정적이고 확정적인**

> The evidence is not yet conclusive.

논문에서 매우 유용한 표현입니다.

> A single experiment is rarely conclusive.

반대 개념으로는 `preliminary`, `inconclusive`도 기억해두세요.

### 3. confidence interval

**신뢰구간**

> The confidence interval is relatively wide, suggesting considerable uncertainty around the estimate.

핵심은 복잡한 통계 설명보다 이 의미를 말할 수 있는 것입니다.

> A narrower confidence interval generally indicates a more precise estimate.

### 4. estimate

**추정치 / 추정하다**

> The estimated improvement is approximately two percentage points.

논문에서는 관찰한 숫자를 무조건 정확한 진실처럼 말하기보다 `estimate`를 자주 사용합니다.

> We estimate that the method reduces inference cost by around 15%.

### 5. uncertainty

**불확실성**

> We should report the uncertainty around the performance estimate.

일상 영어에서도 사용 가능합니다.

> There is still considerable uncertainty about the final cost.

자주 쓰는 조합은 `considerable uncertainty`, `remaining uncertainty`, `uncertainty around an estimate`입니다.

## 4. Correction Exercise — 3분

먼저 직접 고쳐본 뒤 정답을 확인하세요.

### 1

> I have confidence that this method is surely better.

더 자연스럽게:

> I am reasonably confident that this method is better.

더 신중하게:

> I am reasonably confident that the method provides some improvement.

설명: `have confidence that`도 가능하지만 이 문장에서는 지나치게 딱딱하고, `surely`까지 붙이면 확신 표현이 중복됩니다.

* surely better △
* reasonably confident that … ✅

---

### 2

> The result has some uncertainty because every experiment was different.

더 자연스럽게:

> There is some uncertainty because the results varied across runs.

설명: 영어에서는 보통 `result has uncertainty`보다 **there is uncertainty**라고 말합니다. 또 실험 자체가 달랐다는 뜻이 아니라 결과가 흔들렸다면 `the results varied across runs`가 정확합니다.

---

### 3

> The accuracy increased about 2 percentage points.

정답:

> The accuracy increased by about 2 percentage points.

설명: 변화량에는 `by`를 사용합니다.

* increased **by** 2 percentage points
* increased **to** 85%
* increased **from** 83% **to** 85%

이 세 가지를 구분하세요.

---

### 4

> We cannot confident that the effect is real.

정답:

> We cannot be confident that the effect is real.

또는:

> We cannot yet be confident that the effect is real.

설명: `confident`는 형용사이므로 `be`가 필요합니다.

* cannot confident ❌
* cannot be confident ✅

`yet`을 넣으면 “현재 근거로는 아직”이라는 의미가 되어 연구 영어에 특히 자연스럽습니다.

---

### 5

> More experiments will make the result more sure.

더 자연스럽게:

> More experiments would increase our confidence in the result.

또는:

> Additional experiments would provide a more precise estimate of the effect.

설명: 영어에서는 `make the result more sure`라고 하지 않습니다. **사람의 confidence가 올라가거나 estimate의 precision이 높아진다**고 표현합니다.

---

## 5. 60–90 Second Speaking Practice — 2분

다음 흐름으로 말해보세요.

> The method improved accuracy by approximately …
> The same general trend appeared across …
> Therefore, I am reasonably confident that …
> However, there is still some uncertainty regarding …
> The estimate may be imprecise because …
> I would want to examine … before making a stronger claim.
> Based on the available evidence, I would conclude that …

오늘은 `maybe`를 최대한 줄이고 다음처럼 **확신의 강도**를 단계적으로 조절해보세요.

> It is possible that the method helps.
> The method may improve performance.
> The evidence suggests that the method improves performance.
> I am reasonably confident that the method improves performance.
> The evidence strongly supports the conclusion that the method improves performance.

마지막 문장으로 갈수록 더 강한 근거가 필요합니다. **고급 영어는 강하게 말하는 능력이 아니라, 근거에 맞는 강도로 말하는 능력**입니다.

## Compact Model Answer — 1분

> The new method improves accuracy by approximately two percentage points on average, and the same general trend appears across several runs. For that reason, I am reasonably confident that the method provides a genuine benefit. However, there is still some uncertainty regarding the exact size of the improvement because the results vary across random seeds. I would therefore avoid describing the evidence as conclusive. Before making a stronger claim, I would examine the confidence interval, increase the number of runs, and test the method on additional datasets. Based on the available evidence, I would conclude that the method is promising and probably beneficial, rather than claiming that it is consistently superior in every setting.

오늘 입에 붙일 세 문장은 이것입니다: **“I am reasonably confident that …” → “There is still some uncertainty regarding …” → “The evidence is encouraging, but not yet conclusive.”**
