# Day 18 — Challenging an Assumption Without Sounding Confrontational

오늘 목표는 **상대의 가정이나 전제를 직접적으로 공격하지 않으면서, 논리적으로 검토하고 대안을 제시하는 것**입니다. 연구 미팅, 코드 리뷰, 기술 면접에서 매우 자주 필요한 능력이고, C1 수준에서는 단순한 `I disagree`보다 **assumption → concern → evidence → alternative** 구조로 말하는 것이 중요합니다.

## 1. Speaking Prompt — 2분

다음 질문에 60–90초 동안 영어로 답해보세요.

> Your team assumes that using a larger model will automatically improve the final system. You are not convinced. How would you challenge this assumption professionally?

답변에는 다음 내용을 포함하세요.

1. 현재 팀이 전제로 삼고 있는 것
2. 왜 그 전제가 항상 맞지는 않을 수 있는지
3. 어떤 근거를 확인해야 하는지
4. 더 나은 의사결정 방법

시작 문장으로는 다음이 좋습니다.

> I think that assumption is worth examining more carefully.

---

## 2. Reusable Speaking Templates — 3분

### Template 1: 전제 자체를 검토하기

> I think we may be assuming that **[assumption]**, but that may not hold under **[condition]**.

예:

> I think we may be assuming that a larger model will always perform better, but that may not hold under strict latency constraints.

`Your assumption is wrong.`보다 훨씬 자연스럽고 협업적인 표현입니다.

### Template 2: 근거 요구하기

> Before accepting that assumption, I would want to see evidence that **[claim]**.

예:

> Before accepting that assumption, I would want to see evidence that the larger model provides a meaningful improvement on our actual workload.

### Template 3: 더 나은 검증 방법 제안하기

> Rather than assuming **[claim]**, we could test whether **[question]** by **[experiment]**.

예:

> Rather than assuming that scale is beneficial, we could test whether the accuracy gain justifies the additional latency and memory usage.

---

## 3. Useful Words & Expressions — 4분

### 1. assumption

**가정, 전제**

> The conclusion depends on the assumption that the training and production data have similar distributions.

논문에서도 매우 자주 등장합니다.

> This assumption may not hold in real-world settings.

여기서 `hold`는 “성립하다”라는 뜻입니다.

---

### 2. take something for granted

**~을 당연하다고 여기다**

> We should not take it for granted that more parameters always lead to better performance.

일상 영어에서도 자주 씁니다.

> I used to take reliable internet access for granted.

기술 문맥에서는 **검증되지 않은 전제를 당연하게 받아들이지 말자**는 뜻으로 활용하기 좋습니다.

---

### 3. premise

**논리적 전제**

> The argument is based on the premise that compute is not a constraint.

`assumption`과 비슷하지만 `premise`는 특히 **논리적 주장이나 논증의 출발점**이라는 느낌이 강합니다.

> If the premise is incorrect, the conclusion may not follow.

---

### 4. hold

**성립하다, 여전히 참이다**

> This relationship may hold for large datasets but not for smaller ones.

연구 영어에서 매우 유용합니다.

> It remains unclear whether the finding holds across different domains.

`hold true`라고도 합니다.

> The same pattern holds true for multilingual models.

---

### 5. warrant

**~을 정당화할 만큼 충분하다**

> The current evidence does not warrant such a strong conclusion.

또는:

> A 1% improvement may not warrant doubling the inference cost.

이미 배운 `justify`와 매우 비슷하지만, `warrant`는 **근거가 어떤 결론이나 행동을 정당화하는가**를 말할 때 자주 사용합니다.

---

## 4. Correction Exercise — 3분

먼저 직접 고쳐본 뒤 정답을 확인하세요.

### 1

> I think your assumption is wrong.

더 자연스럽게:

> I am not sure that assumption holds in this case.

또는:

> I think that assumption deserves closer examination.

설명: 문법적으로 틀린 문장은 아니지만, 회의에서는 너무 직접적일 수 있습니다. **사람이 아니라 전제의 타당성**을 검토하는 식으로 바꾸면 훨씬 전문적으로 들립니다.

---

### 2

> We cannot take as granted that bigger models are better.

정답:

> We cannot take it for granted that bigger models are better.

설명: 고정 표현입니다.

* take it for granted that ... ✅
* take something for granted ✅

예:

> We should not take low latency for granted.

---

### 3

> This assumption may not be correct in all situation.

정답:

> This assumption may not be correct in all situations.

더 자연스럽게:

> This assumption may not hold in all situations.

설명: `all` 뒤의 가산명사는 일반적으로 복수형입니다.

* all situation ❌
* all situations ✅

연구 영어에서는 `may not hold`가 특히 자연스럽습니다.

---

### 4

> We need evidence which the larger model is actually better.

정답:

> We need evidence that the larger model is actually better.

설명: `evidence` 뒤에 완전한 절이 오면 `that`을 씁니다.

> evidence that the method works

`which`는 앞의 명사를 수식하는 관계대명사이므로 여기서는 맞지 않습니다.

---

### 5

> Before decide, we should test the model in real environment.

정답:

> Before deciding, we should test the model in a real-world environment.

또는:

> Before making a decision, we should evaluate the model under real-world conditions.

설명:

* before decide ❌
* before deciding ✅

그리고 기술 영어에서는 `real environment`보다 다음 표현이 더 자연스럽습니다.

* real-world environment
* production environment
* real-world conditions

---

## 5. 60–90 Second Speaking Practice — 2분

다음 흐름을 따라 소리 내어 말해보세요.

> I think we may be assuming that …
> However, I am not sure that assumption holds when …
> One concern is that …
> Before accepting that premise, I would want to see evidence that …
> The current evidence may not warrant …
> Rather than taking … for granted, we could …
> Based on those results, we could then decide whether …

오늘은 특히 **직접적인 반대 표현을 부드럽게 바꾸는 것**을 연습하세요.

초급식:

> I disagree.

조금 더 자연스럽게:

> I am not fully convinced that this is the best approach.

한 단계 더:

> I think the conclusion depends on an assumption that may not hold under our current constraints.

이 마지막 문장은 회의에서 굉장히 유용합니다. 의견 충돌을 **논리 구조의 검토**로 바꿔주기 때문입니다.

## Compact Model Answer — 1분

> I think we may be assuming that a larger model will automatically lead to a better system, but I am not sure that assumption holds in every setting. A larger model may improve accuracy, but it can also increase latency, memory usage, and operating cost. Before accepting the premise that scale is always beneficial, I would want to see evidence that the performance gain is meaningful on our actual workload. I would also compare the models under the same evaluation conditions. Rather than taking the benefits of scale for granted, we could benchmark both models and evaluate accuracy, latency, memory consumption, and cost. If the larger model provides a substantial improvement without violating our system constraints, then the additional complexity may be warranted.

오늘 입에 붙일 세 문장은 이것입니다: **“I’m not sure that assumption holds.” → “Before accepting that premise, I would want to see evidence that …” → “We should not take it for granted that …”**
